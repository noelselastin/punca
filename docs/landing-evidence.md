# Landing-page evidence notes

Checked 9 November 2026 against the sources linked in `THE PROBLEM google hackathon.docx`. The document is research input, not evidence that Punca has already improved student outcomes.

## Included on the page

- **Introductory courses and degree completion:** Hatfield, Brown & Topaz (2022), *Do introductory courses disproportionately drive minoritized students out of STEM pathways?*, PNAS Nexus 1(4), pgac167. [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9802213/) / [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgac167). Nearly 110,000 records from six US public research universities. The 48% to 33% comparison is a modelled probability for a STEM-intending white male student with average preparation: C-or-better grades in all introductory STEM courses versus below C in one or more. For a similarly prepared underrepresented minority female student the corresponding values are 35% and 21%. It is an association, not a causal estimate or a Punca intervention result. The page states the cohort, comparison and limitation beside the headline statistic. This is **2022 PNAS Nexus**, not 2023 PNAS.
- **Class size and feedback:** [UNSW, Assessing Large Classes](https://www.teaching.unsw.edu.au/assessing-large-classes). The guidance describes classes of 1,000–2,000 as increasingly common and lists high-quality individual feedback and workload among assessment challenges. This is teaching guidance, not an APU class-size measurement.
- **Prior knowledge:** [National Academies (2025), Transforming Undergraduate STEM Education, chapter 3](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/28268/chapter/5), page 46. The quotation on the landing page is the exact 19-word sentence beginning “New knowledge is generated…” and ending “…prior knowledge and experience.” The quotation is research, not a student testimonial.
- **AI diagnosis:** [Li, Morley & Zaki (2025), Mind the Knowledge Gap](https://easychair.org/publications/preprint/4C1J). Identifying prior knowledge and misconceptions is described as under-explored. The page identifies the work as a preprint and does not turn it into a quantified comparison with Punca or a universal claim about chatbots.

## Excluded from the supplied draft

These numbers were withheld because the cited material did not substantiate them in the earlier review, or no supporting source was supplied. They should not be used in the pitch without new verification:

| Draft claim | Reason for exclusion |
| --- | --- |
| Over 60% of STEM dropouts occur in the first two years | The linked ACM DOI, `10.1145/3170358.3170397`, identifies a different study about response times and self-regulation. |
| Gaps are noticed after 4–6 weeks and catching up is statistically improbable | The cited Koriat & Bjork (2005) work concerns study-time overconfidence; the draft's timeline was not established. |
| Under 15% use office hours, while over 80% report stress | DOI `10.1187/cbe.24-08-0214` identifies Dauber & Hsu (2025), a qualitative interview study with 20 students, not the attributed Griffin survey. Those percentages were not established. |
| 20%+ DFW rates caused by hidden gaps | No source was given for the number or the proposed causal relationship. |

## Product claims

The flight is an illustrative interaction, not a fabricated student record. Joining a circle does not earn a stamp; a passed re-check reaching Solid does. Lecturer slots require confirmation. The current prototype covers 37 curated concepts and uses synthetic demonstration students. Real-student learning outcomes have not been measured. The retained Gemini evaluation is separately documented in README section 15 and is not presented as proof of educational effectiveness.
